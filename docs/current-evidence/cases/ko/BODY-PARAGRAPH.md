A knockout seems better than merely damaging a target, but the attack scorer also values which target it hits. Our experimental Lucario policy could prefer a high-value partial hit over a projected knockout. We tested a large knockout-priority bonus against seven fixed opponent implementations, retaining 8,400 games: 600 per arm and opponent, balanced between seats, with draws worth half a win. The same bonus changed mean game score by +14.33 percentage points against prvsiyan’s Static-Deck Tusk v24 and −11.58 against penguin069’s Public Scores 915; their approximate 95% intervals were [+8.84, +19.82] and [−17.13, −6.04]. Two other implementations had negative intervals; three crossed zero. These exploratory comparisons support a narrower lesson than “always take the knockout”: a plausible tactical preference can have opposite whole-game effects. They do not identify which individual decisions caused the losses, isolate deck causality, or demonstrate a successful conditional selector. That left the condition for using the bonus as an unanswered strategy question.
