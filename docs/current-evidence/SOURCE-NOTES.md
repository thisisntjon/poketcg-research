## [6] Deck-policy counter and conditional value

[Methods](numerical/METHODS.md), [input provenance](numerical/SOURCE-MANIFEST.json) and the [verifier](numerical/verify_results.py) reproduce the current game-result arithmetic. The 28,000 development rows include an 8,000-game factorial and the separate 4,000-row second-machine reproduction. Targeting pools two comparisons; card and combined each pool three, using within-batch controls, decided games and inverse-variance pooling on the stated 40%-Alakazam panel. Gains are +3.62, +5.02 and +7.82 [6.08, 9.55] percentage points. All three combined comparisons are positive. Shared factorial arms make the pooled estimates dependent; the interaction does not establish synergy. Direct rounding gives 9.55, correcting the historical double-rounded 9.56 endpoint.

The distinct 8,400-row panel has seven fixed implementations, 600 games per arm/opponent, 300 per seat and win/draw/loss scores 1/0.5/0. Current Figure 2 gives absolute scores and changes: +20.42 and +18.75 for two Alakazam implementations; the other five average −0.47 [−2.64, +1.71]. Normal approximate intervals use unbiased sample variance/n and condition on fixed implementations and independent games, arms and cells. Analyses are descriptive without multiplicity adjustment. One Alakazam deck overlaps development under another pilot; the panel's Grimmsnarl pilot is not submitted c61.

The [historical sensitivity supplement](review-supplement/README.md) retains the separate random-effects analysis excluding the development-overlapping deck. Under draws scored as half, its historical calculation gives +2.72 [−3.29, +8.74] percentage points; the interval includes zero. That analysis uses its historical p(1−p)/n variance approximation, distinct from the current chart's sample-score variance. Its pre-run declaration chronology is qualified in the supplement; the article makes no claim that the finalized analysis was registered before any games began.

[Opponent identities](numerical/opponents.json) and the [additional retained-file inspection](numerical/retained-opponent-identity.json) identify available aliases and hashes without reconstructing every historical execution. The separate 16,000-game exact-c61 comparison has control W/L/D 3,463/4,508/29 and treatment 3,457/4,526/17. Decided-game change is −0.14 [−1.68, +1.40]; this establishes neither equivalence nor absence of cost. Terminal outcomes do not identify which individual move sequences caused each win.

The experimental Lucario lineage is makthanithin's Apache-2.0 community_1084 policy; the author's intervention is the card substitution, targeting modification and experimental comparison. [Mechanics inspection](source-evidence/receipts/source-verification.json) records source identities. Aura Jab and Mega Brave costs, damage, acceleration and reuse constraint were additionally inspected against retained card data, whose SHA-256 is a0ea63cf7adcb65d35436ce0eb390de6e2e35654a7c67c065a45f4abaa00f373. ## [10] Knockout priority: opposite effects across implementations

The [KO case](cases/ko/README.md) includes all 8,400 terminal outcome rows from a September 4 panel and a standard-library verifier. Each of seven implementations has 600 games per arm and 300 per seat. The tested experimental Lucario policy adds 100,000 to a projected-knockout target score with the dedicated switch ON; OFF omits that switch. This preference does not guarantee that every actual knockout dominates every possible alternative.

Scoring win/draw/loss as 1/0.5/0, effects are +14.33 points for prvsiyan Static-Deck Tusk v24; −11.58 for penguin069 Public Scores 915; −7.42 for aristophanivan 2plyexpectimax; and −7.75 for pixiux Mega Lucario v62. All seven cells and absolute scores appear in current Figure 3 and the [complete aggregate table](cases/ko/aggregates.json). The other three intervals cross zero. Approximate 95% intervals use independent-game sample-score variance with no multiplicity adjustment. These are exploratory, fixed-implementation comparisons; seed independence and extra variation are not established by the retained records.

The [claim contract](cases/ko/CLAIM-CONTRACT.json) keeps this panel separate from the counter's different 8,400 rows, older KO pools and conditional follow-up. Original JSONLs and metadata were re-read during export, matching every exported outcome. A two-commit label difference has an identical tracked agent tree and eval.py. The [identity export](cases/ko/identities.json) distinguishes historical recorded settings from current retained-file inspections. Historical pool hashes describe alias/selfcheck text rather than opponent source bytes. No complete engine/runtime recreation, prospective custody, individual-move mechanism, causal deck explanation or successful conditional selector is established.
